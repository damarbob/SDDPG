# 0049 - Multi-Worker Liberator, Excluded at Page-Table Granularity

**Status:** Proposed
**Created:** 2026-09-15

## Context

ADR [`0009`](0009-tombstone-based-slot-eviction.md) fixes the Liberator as a strict process-level singleton and, in its own Consequences, names the cost explicitly: "the singleton model means total sweep throughput is bounded by one process. Operators with extreme tombstone churn must accept longer reclaim latency rather than horizontally scaling the daemon." ADR [`0046`](0046-a-gapped-sweep-skips-the-chunk-and-does-not-reclaim.md) sharpened that cost further without meaning to: a permanently-gapping slot keeps its place at the head of `tombstoned_at ASC` and can starve every tombstone behind it, up to `liberatorBatchSize`, for as long as it keeps gapping — a queueing problem a single process cannot route around.

The project roadmap carried this as item 1 under "Shared-hosting deployment mode": `src/Liberator/TombstonedSlotRepository.php` still said outright that there is "No `FOR UPDATE` claim: the Liberator is a strict singleton," and that remained true of both `bin/stardust liberator` and ADR [`0048`](0048-bounded-combined-tick-for-cron-driven-hosting.md)'s `CombinedTick` — each is one Liberator at a time by construction, closing a narrower worry the shared-hosting work had raised (a URL-driven or post-response Liberator having no `flock` protection) without touching the actual question: an operator who wants *more than one* Liberator running concurrently for reclaim throughput.

**This decision reverses part of an `Accepted` ADR, which is the main thing that makes it non-routine.** ADR 0009 does not merely assume a singleton — its Operational Parameters pin it as a fixed runtime profile, and its Rejected Alternatives names and rejects the specific mechanism this ADR reaches for instead:

> Multi-worker Liberator coordinated by `SKIP LOCKED` on `stardust_slot_assignments` — rejected because the sweep workload is IO-bound (bulk `UPDATE` on a single page) rather than CPU-bound, so additional workers do not increase throughput. They do, however, multiply InnoDB row-lock contention against concurrent reads on the swept page and fragment the per-slot `sweep_cursor_id` semantics. The marginal throughput gain does not justify the loss of a clean single-cursor sweep model.

ADR [`0029`](0029-liberator-sweep-omits-tenant-predicate.md) (also `Accepted`) re-ratifies the singleton in its scoping sentence: "the singleton model … remains in force unchanged." This ADR answers 0009's objections on their merits — at a different granularity than the alternative it rejected — rather than asserting them away, and leaves both ADRs' text in place per the append-only convention, with dated pointers added at the end of this document.

## Decision

**Exclusion moves from the process (a PID file) to the page table (`GET_LOCK('stardust_sweep_page_{pageId}', 0)`), and the Liberator becomes multi-worker like the Reconciler and the Chronicler: run N processes, get up-to-N-way reclaim throughput, bounded by the number of distinct pages actually holding tombstones.**

**Commitment 1 — one worker at a time per `entry_slots_page_N` table, via a named advisory lock with a zero-second timeout.** `Liberator\SweepPageLock::tryAcquire(int $pageId): ?AdvisoryLock` wraps a new `AdvisoryLock::tryAcquire(PDO, string $name, int $timeoutSeconds = 0): ?self` — the non-throwing sibling of the existing `AdvisoryLock::acquire()`, mirroring `PidFileGuard::tryAcquire()`'s contract exactly: contention (`GET_LOCK` returns `0`) returns `null` instead of throwing; a server-side error (`NULL`) still throws, because a skip and a broken server must not look the same to the caller. The timeout is hard-coded `0`, not a `Config` field, on the same normative-constant posture as the Watcher's `GET_LOCK('stardust_page_provision', 10)`: a Liberator worker that cannot take one page's lock has other pages it could be sweeping instead, so waiting is never the right move.

**Commitment 2 — this is deliberately not `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on `stardust_slot_assignments`, the exact mechanism ADR 0009 rejected, and not a per-slot advisory lock either.** Both were reconsidered and both fail for reasons specific to this workload, not for the generic reasons 0009 gave:

1. *A row lock cannot span the unit of work.* `SlotSweeper::sweep()` runs many short transactions per slot — one `beginTransaction()`/`commit()` per chunk in `commitChunk()`. A `FOR UPDATE` claim taken once in `loadBatch()` would die at the first commit, guarding only the batch read and nothing after it. Making a row lock span the whole sweep would need lease columns, heartbeats, and abandoned-claim recovery on `stardust_slot_assignments` — the Chronicler's machinery, and new DDL this ADR deliberately avoids (see Commitment 6).
2. *Slot granularity is the wrong unit, and a per-slot advisory lock would recreate exactly the problem page granularity solves.* `commitChunk()` issues `UPDATE {tableName} SET {slotColumn} = NULL WHERE entry_id IN (…)`. Two workers sweeping two *different slots of the same page* both touch the *same rows* of the same table, taking conflicting InnoDB record locks — precisely ADR 0009's "multiply InnoDB row-lock contention" objection. Post-ADR-0046 this is worse than merely slow: contention burns the bounded deadlock-retry budget, takes a gap, and a gapped sweep **does not reclaim** (ADR 0046). Slot-level parallelism would silently convert reclamations into deferrals.

Page-table granularity answers all three of ADR 0009's original objections without reintroducing either failure mode:

| 0009's objection | Answer at page granularity |
| :-- | :-- |
| "additional workers do not increase throughput (IO-bound)" | True *within* one page. Parallelism here is **across** page tables — distinct tables, distinct IO. The honest throughput bound is the number of distinct pages holding tombstones, not the worker count, and this ADR makes no stronger claim than that. |
| "multiply InnoDB row-lock contention" | Two workers never touch the same page table concurrently, so no Liberator-vs-Liberator row contention is possible. Liberator-vs-reader contention on a page is unchanged from today and stays throttled by the existing `liberatorInterChunkDelayMicros`. |
| "fragment the per-slot `sweep_cursor_id` semantics" | The cursor stays strictly single-writer: exactly one worker ever sweeps a given slot at a time, so ADR 0046's durable cursor-pinning invariant and blueprint AC#9's crash-resumption property are untouched. |

**Commitment 3 — one slot at a time, acquire-sweep-release, never a whole batch's locks up front.** `Liberator::sweepBatch()` walks its loaded batch of tombstoned slots one at a time: try the slot's page lock; on contention, count it and move to the next slot; on success, sweep the slot immediately and release its page lock — in a `finally`, so a thrown exception cannot leak it — before considering the next slot. **A batch is not pre-locked wholesale.** `TombstonedSlotRepository::loadBatch()` can return slots spanning many distinct pages (up to `liberatorBatchSize`, default 50); a design that acquired every claimable slot's page lock before sweeping any of them would let one worker monopolize every page in its batch for the whole cycle, starving every other worker for that entire cycle and defeating the reason this ADR exists. This was found during implementation, by hand-testing two real `bin/stardust liberator` processes against one database and observing that an "acquire-all-then-sweep-all" first draft let one worker win an entire multi-page batch before a second, independently-started process could get its first tick in — see Verification.

**Commitment 4 — no process-level singleton enforcement remains.** `bin/stardust liberator` drops its `PidFileGuard::acquire(..., LiberatorSingletonViolationException::class)` call entirely, the same shape as `reconciler` and `chronicler`; run as many processes as desired. `Daemon\CombinedTick` (ADR 0048) stops taking the Liberator's pid-file lock — it still takes the Watcher's, since the Watcher remains a strict singleton — because the Liberator no longer has one to take: its own `SweepPageLock` acquisitions happen inside `Liberator::sweepBatch()` regardless of what invoked it. **One direct consequence: a `bin/stardust tick` run and a standalone `bin/stardust liberator` process may now run concurrently on the same installation**, where before `tick` taking the Liberator's pid file made every overlapping `liberator` invocation report a harmless no-op skip. They simply divide the tombstoned-slot batch by whichever pages each claims first — `docs/deployment.md`'s "never run `tick` alongside the individual daemons" caution is narrowed to name this one exception. `LiberatorSingletonViolationException` is deleted as unreferenced, on the same footing as ADR 0041's `EntrySearchInterface::supportsSortOn()` addition: the v0.3.0 tag is unissued, so removing public surface is not yet a breaking change.

**Commitment 5 — `sweep_started` moves to the end of the cycle and gains two fields; every other Liberator event gains one.** With N workers, `worker_identity` (`host:pid:uuid`, the existing `Support\WorkerIdentity::mint()` shared with the Chronicler and the Reconciler's import-job source) is minted once per process and rides all five Liberator events, since the event stream alone can no longer distinguish which process emitted what. `sweep_started` additionally carries `slots_claimed` and `slots_contended` alongside the existing `batch_size`, and — because Commitment 3 forbids knowing those tallies before the batch has actually been walked — now fires once at the **end** of a cycle that claimed at least one slot, rather than announcing the batch before any sweeping starts. A fully-contended cycle (every slot's page already held by another worker) emits nothing at all, extending blueprint AC#13's idle-tick silence from "no tombstoned slots" to "no tombstoned slots this worker could actually claim." `tests/Smoke/Liberator/LiberatorSweepTest::testEmitsExpectedEventSequence` pins the resulting single-worker order (`sweep_chunk`(s), `sweep_complete`, `sweep_started`) against the pre-0049 shape, where `sweep_started` led.

**Commitment 6 — no DDL, no `Config` field, no new event name.** Worth stating positively, because each is a place the lease-column alternative (Commitment 2) would have cost something: no `Bootstrapper::ensureXxx()` probe, no `Support\SchemaFixture::CORE_TABLES` change, no `Config` constructor append, no ADR 0020 event-name addition (only new *fields* on existing events, which the closed-vocabulary guard does not gate). `SlotSweeper`, `TombstonedSlot`, and `TombstonedSlotRepository::loadBatch()` are byte-for-byte unchanged in their SQL — `SlotSweeper::sweep()` gained only the `string $workerIdentity` parameter it threads onto its own four events. The ~330 lines of ADR 0009/0045/0046 chunk, gap, and cursor-pinning logic inside `SlotSweeper` are untouched; multi-worker-ness lives entirely one layer up, at whether a slot's page lock was claimed before `sweep()` is ever called.

### New events

None. `worker_identity` is a new *field* on the five existing `liberator`-source events (`sweep_started`, `sweep_chunk`, `sweep_complete`, `deadlock_retry`, `sweep_gap_flagged`), and `sweep_started` gains `slots_claimed` / `slots_contended`; the ADR 0020 closed-vocabulary guard (`tests/Smoke/EventVocabularyTest`) gates event *names*, not fields, so it needs no update. ADR 0020's source-specific-fields line (documenting which extra fields each source adds) is amended to list `worker_identity` for the Liberator.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- Unlocks real horizontal reclaim throughput for the first time since ADR 0009: an operator with extreme tombstone churn can run N `bin/stardust liberator` processes instead of accepting the latency ADR 0009's Consequences named as the singleton's explicit cost.
- Directly answers the ADR 0046 head-of-line problem this ADR's Context raises: a slot stuck gapping on one page's lock no longer blocks every other tombstoned slot behind it in `tombstoned_at ASC` order — a second worker can reach past it to a different page immediately, rather than waiting for the single process to work through the queue.
- No DDL, no new `Config` field, no new event name, no change to `SlotSweeper`'s deadlock/gap/cursor logic (Commitment 6) — the whole design is additive at the daemon-orchestration layer, leaving the harder-won ADR 0045/0046 invariants completely alone.
- Verified empirically against MySQL 8.0.13 before any code was written (see Verification), and the Commitment-3 monopolization defect was caught the same way — by running two real processes by hand — rather than shipped and discovered in production.

**Negative:**

- **This ADR reverses part of an `Accepted` decision.** ADR 0009's singleton framing and its explicit rejection of a multi-worker Liberator predate this ADR by roughly five months of the project's timeline; both are left textually intact per the append-only convention, with dated pointers added below, but a reader who has only read ADR 0009 will not know it has been narrowed until they also read this one. The same applies to ADR 0029's scoping sentence.
- **The hung-but-still-connected worker trade is real and is not new, but it is worth restating precisely.** `GET_LOCK` releases only via `RELEASE_LOCK` or the holding connection's death — it is untouched by `COMMIT`/`ROLLBACK` on the sweep's own per-chunk transactions (this is exactly what makes it safe to wrap a multi-transaction sweep at all; verified directly against 8.0.13 rather than assumed, see Verification). A worker that hangs while remaining connected therefore holds its one page's capacity indefinitely, exactly as the pre-0049 singleton held **all** capacity indefinitely under the same failure — strictly better in blast radius, identically observable via tombstone depth and age, and self-healing the same way: killing the stuck process (or its connection dropping) releases the lock immediately.
- **`sweep_started`'s position in the event stream changed** (Commitment 5): an operator's dashboard or log-parsing tooling that assumed `sweep_started` always leads a cycle's events needs updating. This is a real, if narrow, observability-contract change for existing single-worker deployments upgrading to this ADR, not merely an internal implementation detail.
- One more entry in `src/Daemon/CLAUDE.md`'s "who enforces singletons" table for a future reader to learn as a named exception — the Watcher remains the sole process-level singleton in the engine after this ADR, but the Liberator's *page-level* exclusion is a new middle category (neither a process singleton nor unconstrained multi-worker like the Reconciler/Chronicler) that has to be explained rather than inferred from either precedent.

**Rejected alternatives:**

- **`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on `stardust_slot_assignments`, at slot (row) granularity** — the exact mechanism ADR 0009 originally rejected. Still rejected here, for the reasons in Commitment 2: a row lock cannot span `SlotSweeper`'s many per-chunk transactions without lease-column machinery, and even with such machinery, slot-level parallelism reintroduces InnoDB row-lock contention on the shared page table that a gapped sweep can no longer safely absorb since ADR 0046.
- **Lease columns + heartbeats + abandoned-claim recovery on `stardust_slot_assignments`**, mirroring the Chronicler's design. Rejected: it is the only alternative that could make a row-level claim span the whole sweep, but it costs new DDL (`worker_identity`, `claimed_at`, `heartbeat_at` on a table that currently has none of them), a new abandoned-claim work source, and a lease-timeout `Config` field — all for a claim granularity (the row) that still has the InnoDB-contention problem Commitment 2 describes. Page-table `GET_LOCK` gets equivalent crash-safety (MySQL releases the lock when the connection dies) for none of that cost.
- **A per-slot advisory lock** (`GET_LOCK('stardust_sweep_slot_{id}', 0)`) instead of a per-page one. Rejected for the same reason as `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` at slot granularity: two workers holding locks on two different slots of the same page would still both issue conflicting `UPDATE … WHERE entry_id IN (…)` statements against that page's rows. The lock's granularity has to match the granularity of the actual contention, which is the page table, not the registry row that merely names a column on it.
- **Acquiring every claimable slot's page lock before sweeping any of them** (an "acquire-all-then-sweep-all" shape, rather than Commitment 3's one-at-a-time loop). This was the first implementation, not merely a design considered and set aside on paper: it passed every automated test, because a single-worker test cannot distinguish "locks held briefly and sequentially" from "locks held for the whole batch's duration." It was caught only by hand-testing two real `bin/stardust liberator` processes against a deliberately uneven two-page fixture and observing that the process which ticked first won *both* pages before the second process's tick could run at all — silently defeating the throughput goal for any batch spanning more than one page. Rejected once found; Commitment 3 is the fix, and the by-hand method that caught it is recorded in Verification so a future regression of the same shape is caught the same way.
- **Threading a per-run `correlation_id` across workers**, the way ADR 0048's `tick` mints one id for its own three events. Not applicable here: each Liberator process already mints its own per-cycle `correlation_id` exactly as it did before this ADR (one per `sweepBatch()` call), and `worker_identity` is the added dimension that makes N processes' otherwise-identical-looking events distinguishable — a second, cross-worker id would duplicate what `worker_identity` already provides.

## Verification

Before any code was written, four claims about `GET_LOCK` this ADR's design depends on were measured against the real MySQL 8.0.13 floor (the project's standing rule: verify database claims empirically, not by assertion) rather than assumed from documentation:

1. A named lock survives `COMMIT` and `ROLLBACK` on the holding connection — confirmed: a lock taken, then committed, then rolled back, remained held (a sibling session's `GET_LOCK(name, 0)` returned `0` after each) until an explicit `RELEASE_LOCK`. This is what makes it safe for one lock to wrap `SlotSweeper::sweep()`'s many per-chunk transactions.
2. One connection can hold many distinct named locks at once — confirmed: three locks taken on one session were all simultaneously visible as held to a sibling session.
3. `GET_LOCK(name, 0)` returns `0` immediately on contention, not after waiting — confirmed: a contended call returned in under 1 ms, matching the Commitment 1 design (a worker with other pages to sweep must not block).
4. Killing the holding connection releases the lock — confirmed: a sibling session's `GET_LOCK` on the same name returned `1` (free) within one poll after the holder's connection was killed.

Then, after implementation:

- `vendor/bin/phpstan analyse` — level 8, zero errors, no baseline.
- The full smoke suite (852 tests) green, including the five new `tests/Smoke/Liberator/LiberatorMultiWorkerTest` cases and the updated `LiberatorSweepTest` event-order assertion.
- **Neutered validation**: `SweepPageLock::tryAcquire()` was temporarily changed to mint a unique lock name per call (so no two callers could ever actually contend), and the four contention-dependent new tests went red as expected; the fifth (lock-released-after-a-sweep) passed vacuously, which is the correct outcome for a neuter that removes contention entirely rather than removing the release path.
- **By hand, against a real database, with real OS processes** — the headline proof, and the one that caught the Commitment 3 defect:
  - Two tombstoned slots seeded on two different pages, one page loaded with many rows (so a worker sweeping it stays busy, and holding its page lock, for several seconds) and the other with few. Two independent `bin/stardust liberator`-equivalent processes launched against the same database: the second process's own `sweep_started` event recorded `batch_size: 2, slots_claimed: 1, slots_contended: 1` — it saw both tombstoned slots, found the busy page's lock held by the first process, skipped it, and reclaimed the other page's slot on its own. Both slots reached `free` once the first process finished its long sweep; page-1's rows were never touched by the second process at any point.
  - Two tombstoned slots seeded on the **same** page: the first process to tick claimed and swept both (the reentrant `GET_LOCK` behaviour within one session, confirmed by claim 2 above, is exactly what makes sweeping several same-page slots in one cycle correct rather than a repeated self-contention); the second process's log carried no events at all for that cycle — the silent-when-idle behaviour Commitment 5 specifies, distinguishing "genuinely nothing to do" from an error.
