# 0050 - Chronicler Cooperative Yield At A Chunk Boundary

**Status:** Proposed
**Created:** 2026-09-16

## Context

ADR [`0048`](0048-bounded-combined-tick-for-cron-driven-hosting.md) shipped a bounded, budget-limited combined run — `StarDust::tick()` / `bin/stardust tick` — composing the Watcher, Liberator and Reconciler over one connection, and deliberately excluded the Chronicler: `ExportJobProcessor::process()` claims one job and runs `while (true)` until that job reaches a terminal state, with no point at which it can hand control back before then. Composing it into a bounded run would either make the budget a lie (the job keeps running past it) or require building a cooperative yield first. Commitment 6 of that ADR named the yield as explicit future work rather than folding it in under time pressure, and until it landed `docs/deployment.md` said so in those words: async exports are unsupported on a cron-only deployment.

Every precondition that ADR named is now in place. ADR [`0047`](0047-the-export-resume-anchor-is-the-artifact-plus-its-byte-offset.md) made `stardust_export_jobs` a genuine, byte-verified resume anchor: `last_cursor`, `artifact_path` and `artifact_bytes` are written on **every** chunk commit (not only the final one), an abandoned re-claim no longer deletes the prior partial, and a re-claim only trusts `last_cursor` once the artifact stream itself reports a verified re-open. That had to land first, for exactly the reason 0048 gave: a yield built on the pre-0047 resume path — which trusted `last_cursor` alone and deleted the partial on every re-claim — would have manufactured 0047's data-loss case deliberately, on every tick boundary, rather than by accident.

## Decision

**At a committed, non-final chunk boundary, `ExportJobProcessor::process()` optionally returns a claimed job to `pending` with its resume anchor intact, rather than continuing to the next chunk.**

**Commitment 1 — the yield point is after a committed chunk, never before one, and never on the final chunk.** The check happens immediately after the chunk's `flush()`, using the same `!$isFinal` guard that already exists for every other post-commit branch. Two consequences follow directly: a yield always carries at least one chunk of durable progress (so a caller handing this a zero-length budget cannot spin the job between `processing` and `pending` without ever advancing it), and the last chunk always completes the job (so a job that fits in one remaining chunk cannot get stuck one boundary short of `completed`). Together these bound convergence: a job of N chunks needs at most N ticks to complete regardless of how aggressively the caller yields.

**Commitment 2 — the yield is a `YieldSignal`, checked once per eligible chunk, reporting a closed-taxonomy cause rather than a bool.** `interface YieldSignal { yieldCause(): ?string; }`, with `null` meaning "keep going" and any other string a request to yield at the next opportunity, carrying its own reason (`'budget'` | `'shutdown'`) into the terminal event without a second probe. This is the same shape as `ShutdownSignal` deliberately widened by one field, not a new pattern: `DeadlineYield` wraps a clock and a fixed deadline timestamp (`'budget'` once reached), `ShutdownYield` adapts an existing `ShutdownSignal` (`'shutdown'`), and `CompositeYield` OR-composes any number of probes, mirroring `CompositeShutdownSignal` exactly.

**Commitment 3 — the yield commit folds into the SAME transaction as the ordinary chunk commit, reusing the existing lease-loss detector unmodified.** The UPDATE gains two clauses over the existing non-final chunk commit — `status = 'pending', worker_identity = NULL` — while `last_cursor`, `heartbeat_at`, `skip_count`, `artifact_path` and `artifact_bytes` are written exactly as any other chunk commit, and the `WHERE id = ? AND worker_identity = ?` self-check is untouched. This means a yield racing a genuine concurrent re-claim resolves exactly the way an ordinary chunk commit racing one does today: `rowCount() === 0` ⇒ `lease_lost`, the (already-closed, see Commitment 4) artifact is left on disk untouched, and terminal-state ownership transfers to the re-claimer — no new race to reason about, because it is the same race. `claimed_at` and `correlation_id` are untouched (no UPDATE clause names them), so an operator still sees when the job was first claimed, and a re-claim continues emitting under the same correlation id.

**Commitment 4 — the artifact's byte anchor is captured BEFORE any `close()`, and `close()` runs BEFORE the commit.** Both halves are load-bearing and independently necessary:

- *Capture before close.* `JsonArtifactStream::close()` writes the trailing `]` through the same `writeRaw()` that increments `bytesWritten()`. Capturing `path()` / `bytesWritten()` after `close()` would anchor the terminator byte into the committed `artifact_bytes`; the next resume's `ftruncate()` would then keep that `]` and append the next row's bytes directly after it, producing invalid JSON. `CsvArtifactStream::close()` has no equivalent write and would not exhibit this — which is exactly why a CSV-only test would not catch it. The anchor is therefore always captured immediately after `flush()`, before any close is even considered, and the same captured values are used both for the commit and for the `chunk_written` event's byte accounting, so the two can never disagree.
- *Close before commit.* Releasing the file's exclusive lock before the row becomes claimable means a resumer's `open()` verification never encounters `restart_cause: 'locked'` — the one rejection cause ADR 0047 reserved for "another process may still be actively writing this file." A yield is the inverse of an abandoned claim in this one respect: an abandoned claim's prior worker may still be alive and holding the lock (which is why `locked` exists at all), while a yield's prior worker has, by construction, already released it before the row it describes becomes visible to anyone else.

A terminator write that itself trips `ChroniclerArtifactDiskFullException` (the trailing `]` won't fit) is caught and ignored on the yield path specifically: the anchor already captured describes bytes the filesystem genuinely took, so the yield still commits cleanly, and the next resume's `ftruncate()` trims past whatever partial terminator made it to disk; the very next `appendRow()` on the resumed stream will trip disk-full properly if the condition persists.

**Commitment 5 — the claimer's pending path gains the SAME anchor-forwarding the abandoned path already had, and reports a distinct claim kind.** Before this change, `ExportJobClaimer::claimPending()` selected neither `last_cursor` nor the anchor columns and hardcoded `lastCursor: null` on the returned `ClaimedJob`. Left unfixed, a yielded job re-claimed through the pending path would receive no anchor, `ArtifactStreamFactory` would mint a fresh path, and the export would restart from byte zero on every single tick — orphaning one partial artifact per tick and never converging on any job larger than one chunk's worth of budget. This was the sketch this ADR's precursor discussion originally got wrong, and it is the entire reason Commitment 5 exists as its own numbered commitment rather than a footnote: **a `pending` row carrying a usable anchor (a non-null `artifact_path` plus a positive `artifact_bytes` — the identical predicate `ArtifactStreamFactory::from()` already applies to decide whether to attempt adoption at all) is not a fresh submission; it is a previously-yielded job.** `claimPending()` now selects and forwards all three columns exactly as `claimAbandoned()` does, and reports `ClaimKind::Resumed` rather than `Pending` when the anchor is usable — giving an operator watching `job_claimed` a direct signal that a job at, say, 40% complete is mid-yield-cycle rather than freshly queued, where `pending` alone would have looked identical to a job that had never run. A genuinely fresh submission has both anchor columns NULL and still claims as `Pending`.

**Commitment 6 — `bin/stardust tick --exports` composes the Chronicler last in a round, and only when explicitly opted in.** `CombinedTick` takes a `Chronicler` as a required constructor dependency (so every existing `combinedTick()` caller keeps working unchanged) but only calls `tickRound()` on it when `run()`'s new `$exports` parameter is `true` — off by default. It runs last in the round, after the Liberator sweep and the Reconciler round, so registry maintenance (page provisioning, slot reclamation, the sync-queue / import-job / backfill drain) is never starved by a large export sharing the connection. When enabled, `runLocked()` builds exactly one `CompositeYield(DeadlineYield(…), ShutdownYield(…))` for the whole run — a `DeadlineYield` pinned to this run's own resolved budget deadline, and a `ShutdownYield` wrapping the run's own shutdown probe — and passes the SAME instance to every `Chronicler::tickRound()` call in the loop. This is load-bearing, not an optimisation: unlike the Liberator's batch-bounded sweep and the Reconciler's round-bounded pass, the Chronicler's `process()` loop would otherwise run one claimed job to completion regardless of size, and `CombinedTick`'s existing budget check — which per ADR 0048 Commitment 2 runs only BETWEEN rounds, never mid-round — cannot bound work that all happens inside a single round. The `DeadlineYield` is what makes that promise instead, from inside `process()`'s own chunk loop.

`$exports` defaults to `false` (surfaced as the `bin/stardust tick --exports` flag) for a reason distinct from ADR 0048's own defaults: the disk-pressure gate's account-quota gap (project roadmap item 2 — the gate reads partition-level free space, not a per-account quota some shared hosts enforce above the filesystem layer) is measured-not-verified, and a cron-only host is exactly the deployment this item is most likely to be found on. `--exports` is the operator's explicit acknowledgement, not an assumption this ADR makes on their behalf.

**Commitment 7 — the persistent `bin/stardust chronicler` daemon composes `ShutdownYield` too, sharing one `ShutdownSignal` instance with its poll loop.** `StarDust::chronicler()` takes an appended, optional `?ShutdownSignal $shutdown` and, when given one, wraps it as this Chronicler instance's default yield signal (used whenever `tickRound()` is called with no per-call override — i.e., every call the standalone daemon's `Tickable::tick()` makes). `null` (the default, used by `combinedTick()`) preserves the exact Phase 7 behaviour every existing caller depends on. The CLI's `chronicler` command builds its `ShutdownSignal` once and passes the SAME instance to both `chronicler($shutdown)` and `pollLoop()->run(..., $shutdown, ...)` — sharing matters here, not merely for tidiness, because `StarDust::shutdownSignal()` mints a fresh `CompositeShutdownSignal` on every call, and two independently-minted composites reading the same flag file would still each report correctly, but two independently-constructed `SignalShutdownSignal`s each register their own `pcntl_signal()` handler, and only the instance the poll loop is actually checking would need to see the flip in the general case — passing one instance closes any doubt rather than relying on both being correct by construction. The consequence: a `SIGTERM` to the persistent daemon mid-export now yields at the next chunk boundary rather than either blocking until the job finishes or being killed outright and forcing the next worker to wait out the full lease timeout before an abandoned-claim sweep can re-claim it.

### New events

`job_yielded` (source `chronicler`, level `info`) is emitted immediately after a successful yield commit, in place of the `job_complete` a normal final chunk would emit. Payload: `job_id`, `worker_identity`, `last_cursor`, `artifact_bytes`, `rows_streamed_total` (this worker's attempt only — not cumulative across prior yields, the same limitation `job_complete`'s own `rows_streamed_total` already has across an abandoned re-claim), `skip_count`, `cause` (`'budget'` | `'shutdown'`), `elapsed_ms`. It joins under the same `correlation_id` as every other event of the job — the submission's id when the job carries one, exactly as `job_claimed` / `chunk_written` / `job_complete` already do, since a yield does not end the operation, only this worker's attempt at it. `tick_started` (source `tick`, ADR 0048) gains one sub-field, `exports` (bool), alongside its existing `advisories`.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- Closes the one capability gap ADR 0048 shipped with explicitly acknowledged: async exports become available on the cron-only / no-persistent-process deployment tier, bounded the same way everything else `tick` composes already is.
- The claimer fix (Commitment 5) is a correctness fix independent of whether an operator ever uses `--exports` — it only matters once something can put a job into `pending` with an anchor attached, which before this ADR never happened, so the pre-existing `claimPending()` gap was latent rather than reachable. It became reachable the moment the yield existed, which is why it ships in the same change rather than as a follow-up.
- No change to any Phase 7 default behaviour: `$yieldSignal` defaults to `null` on `Chronicler`, `$exports` defaults to `false` on `tick()`, and `chronicler()`'s `$shutdown` parameter defaults to `null` — every existing caller of every touched entry point keeps its exact current behaviour without passing anything new.
- The lease-loss detector needed zero new logic (Commitment 3) — a yield racing a re-claim is not a new race, it is the existing race, because the yield commit uses the identical `WHERE worker_identity = self` predicate every other chunk commit already does.

**Negative:**

- A yielded job's `rows_streamed_total` (both on `job_yielded` and on the eventual `job_complete`) reports only the claiming worker's own attempt, not a running total across every yield the job has been through — an operator summing per-event counts to reconstruct total rows exported would undercount unless they sum across every `job_yielded` plus the final `job_complete` for a given `job_id`. This mirrors an identical, pre-existing limitation on the abandoned-claim path and was not solved by this ADR; solving it would mean persisting a cumulative counter on the row, which is out of scope here.
- GC remains idle-cycle-only (unchanged by this ADR), so a Chronicler permanently saturated with yielding, in-progress exports never runs its TTL sweep. This was always true under a persistent daemon busy enough to never idle; this ADR makes it reachable on a cron-only host for the first time, since `--exports` is what puts the Chronicler in the tick's rotation at all. Worth a line in `docs/deployment.md` rather than a code change.
- One more parameter threaded through three constructors (`Chronicler`, `CombinedTick`, and `StarDust::chronicler()`/`tick()`) that a future reader has to learn is mutually consistent rather than independently configurable — `CombinedTick`'s per-call override always wins over `Chronicler`'s own constructor default, which is correct but is one more rule.

**Rejected alternatives:**

- **Thread the yield deadline through `Config` as a dedicated field.** Rejected: the deadline is scoped to one `CombinedTick::run()` invocation, not to the engine's construction-time configuration, and `Config` per ADR 0026 holds settings that outlive a single call. `DeadlineYield` is built fresh, from the already-resolved `TickBudget`, inside `runLocked()` itself.
- **A `NeverYield` no-op implementation of `YieldSignal`, used as every default.** Rejected as unnecessary ceremony: `?YieldSignal $yield = null` at both the `Chronicler` constructor and the `process()` call site, with `$yield?->yieldCause()` or `$yield !== null ? … : null` at the one place it is consulted, expresses "never yield" with no additional class and no additional import for the (numerous) callers that want exactly that.
- **Compose the Chronicler into `tick` unconditionally, matching the Watcher/Liberator/Reconciler.** Rejected for this ADR specifically because of the disk-pressure gate's unverified account-quota gap (see Commitment 6) — composing three already-verified daemons unconditionally was the right call in ADR 0048; composing a fourth whose own pre-flight check has a known, unmeasured blind spot on the exact deployment tier this mode targets is not the same decision, and `--exports` keeps it an explicit operator choice until that gate is fixed or verified sufficient.
- **Have the persistent daemon's `ShutdownYield` apply unconditionally, with no opt-out.** Considered and accepted rather than rejected — there is no scenario where blocking a `SIGTERM` until an arbitrarily large export finishes is preferable to yielding at the next chunk boundary, so no configuration knob was added; `chronicler()`'s `$shutdown` parameter defaults to `null` only because a caller that supplies no shutdown signal at all (i.e., every non-CLI caller today) has nothing to wrap.
